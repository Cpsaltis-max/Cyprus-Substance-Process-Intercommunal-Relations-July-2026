Integrated Research Report

Substance, Process and Intercommunal Relations

Greek Cypriot Public Opinion on the Cyprus Issue · July 2026

Purpose

This report integrates the principal findings presented on the July 2026 research platform and in the accompanying newspaper series.

Data and methodology

The final analytical sample contains 824 usable cases. Final v4 weighting provides 779 valid weights calibrated to CYSTAT 2021 age, gender and current district of residence. The effective sample size is 418.7, with a Kish design effect of 1.86 and a design-adjusted precision benchmark of approximately ±4.8 percentage points. Fieldwork took place from 10 to 31 July 2026.

Substance

The dominant contextual finding is dissatisfaction with the status quo: 92.4% judge it unfair. BBF is accepted or tolerated by 75.3%, compared with 70.3% for a unitary state and 33.3% for a two-state solution. The coexistence of substantial tolerance for more than one arrangement cautions against interpreting public attitudes as a single mutually exclusive constitutional choice.

Hopes and concerns

Respondents’ initial answers reveal a mixed set of material, mobility and institutional expectations. Prominent hopes include property return or compensation (36.8%), freedom of movement and settlement (36.3%) and employment opportunities for young people (23.1%). Prominent concerns include refugee return (approximately 52%), political deadlock or dysfunction (41.8%), daily tensions (28.1%) and cost (19.4%).

Process

82.8% prefer an active negotiation route. Restarting from Crans-Montana is selected by 38.7%, citizens’ assemblies by 22.2%, staged or interim implementation by 21.9%, while 17.2% prefer not to restart negotiations.

Intercommunal relations

51.7% report no contact with Turkish Cypriots, 32.4% less-than-monthly contact and 15.9% monthly or more frequent contact. Mean affective warmth is 57.5 on a 0–100 scale. The weighted association between contact and warmth is r=.327. BBF acceptance or tolerance ranges from 70.3% among respondents reporting no contact to 87.4% among those reporting weekly-or-more contact; this pattern is correlational.

Overall interpretation

Taken together, the findings describe a society that remains dissatisfied with the present division, open to renewed negotiations and attentive to questions of institutional functionality, rights, implementation and everyday intercommunal relations. The evidence also highlights the importance of distinguishing between ideal preferences, tolerable compromises and assessments of the current situation.

Ethics

The study received a favourable opinion from the Cyprus National Bioethics Committee (ΕΕΒΚ ΕΠ 2026.01.96).
