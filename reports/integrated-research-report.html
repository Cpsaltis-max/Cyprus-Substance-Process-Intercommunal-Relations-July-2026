Integrated Research Report

Substance, Process and Intercommunal Relations

Greek Cypriot Public Opinion on the Cyprus Issue · July 2026

Purpose

This public report integrates the evidence selected for unrestricted dissemination alongside the newspaper article series. It is intentionally narrower than the complete research programme.

Data and methodology

The final analytical sample contains 824 usable cases. Final v4 weighting provides 779 valid weights calibrated to CYSTAT 2021 age, gender and current district of residence. The effective sample size is 418.7, with a Kish design effect of 1.86 and a design-adjusted precision benchmark of approximately ±4.8 percentage points. Fieldwork took place from 10 to 31 July 2026.

Substance

The dominant contextual finding is dissatisfaction with the status quo: 92.4% judge it unfair. BBF is accepted or tolerated by 75.3%, compared with 70.3% for a unitary state and 33.3% for a two-state solution. The overlap between acceptable or tolerable options cautions against interpreting the public as divided into simple mutually exclusive constitutional camps.

Baseline hopes and concerns

The public platform uses only measurements taken before exposure to the study's pro- and anti-BBF communication material. Baseline hopes include property return or compensation (36.8%), freedom of movement and settlement (36.3%) and employment opportunities for young people (23.1%). Baseline concerns include refugee return (approximately 52%), political deadlock or dysfunction (41.8%), daily tensions (28.1%) and cost (19.4%). No public inference is made here about which messages change these expectations.

Process

82.8% prefer an active negotiation route. Restarting from Crans-Montana is selected by 38.7%, citizen assemblies by 22.2%, while 17.2% prefer not to restart negotiations. These findings indicate that disagreement over substantive outcomes does not translate directly into a preference for diplomatic inactivity.

Intercommunal relations

51.7% report no contact with Turkish Cypriots and 15.9% report monthly or more frequent contact. Mean affective warmth is 57.5 on a 0–100 scale. The weighted association between contact and warmth is r=.327, suggesting that the relational context remains relevant to understanding public attitudes.

Publication boundary

The complete research programme contains additional analyses. The public platform does not publish post-communication shifts, paired message testing, psychological-readiness targeting profiles, persuasion-oriented predictive modelling, political leadership/gender analyses, or presidential conjoint optimisation. Article 4 of the newspaper series remains a stand-alone article and does not have a corresponding detailed public module.

Ethics and interpretation

The study received a favourable opinion from the Cyprus National Bioethics Committee (ΕΕΒΚ ΕΠ 2026.01.96). Results should be interpreted as weighted estimates from the specified research design and fieldwork period, not as deterministic forecasts of referendum or electoral behaviour.
