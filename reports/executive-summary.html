Executive Summary · July 2026

Greek Cypriot Public Opinion on the Cyprus Issue

Summary of the principal findings from the July 2026 survey.

The final weighted analysis covers 824 usable cases, with 779 valid final v4 weights. The findings are organised around substance, hopes and concerns, process, and intercommunal relations.

Substance: 92.4% judge the status quo unfair; 75.3% accept or tolerate BBF; 70.3% accept or tolerate a unitary state; 33.3% accept or tolerate a two-state solution.

Hopes and concerns: prominent initial hopes include property return or compensation (36.8%), freedom of movement and settlement (36.3%) and jobs for young people (23.1%). Concerns include refugee return (approximately 52%), political deadlock or dysfunction (41.8%), daily tensions (28.1%) and cost (19.4%).

Process: 82.8% prefer an active negotiation route; 38.7% select restarting from Crans-Montana, 22.2% citizens’ assemblies, 21.9% staged or interim implementation, and 17.2% prefer not to restart.

Intercommunal relations: 51.7% report no contact with Turkish Cypriots, 32.4% less-than-monthly contact and 15.9% monthly or more frequent contact. Mean warmth is 57.5 on a 0–100 scale, and contact is positively associated with warmth (weighted r=.327).
