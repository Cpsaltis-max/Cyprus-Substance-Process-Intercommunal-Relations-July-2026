Executive Summary · July 2026

Greek Cypriot Public Opinion on the Cyprus Issue

This summary accompanies the public platform and newspaper series.

The final weighted analysis covers 824 usable cases, with 779 valid final v4 weights. The public evidence is organised around substance, baseline hopes and concerns, process, and intercommunal relations.

Substance: 92.4% judge the status quo unfair; 75.3% accept or tolerate BBF; 70.3% accept or tolerate a unitary state; 33.3% accept or tolerate a two-state solution.

Baseline hopes and concerns: before exposure to any pro- or anti-BBF communication, prominent hopes include property return/compensation (36.8%), freedom of movement/settlement (36.3%) and jobs for young people (23.1%). Concerns include refugee return (approximately 52%), political deadlock/dysfunction (41.8%), daily tensions (28.1%) and cost (19.4%).

Process: 82.8% prefer an active negotiation route; 38.7% select restarting from Crans-Montana, 22.2% citizen assemblies, and 17.2% prefer not to restart.

Intercommunal relations: 51.7% report no contact with Turkish Cypriots and 15.9% monthly or more frequent contact. Mean warmth is 57.5 on a 0–100 scale, and contact is positively associated with warmth (weighted r=.327).

The public platform intentionally excludes analyses designed to reveal post-communication movement, message effectiveness, psychological targeting, candidate optimisation or other campaign-strategy applications.
