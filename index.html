Executive Summary

What the public evidence shows

The July 2026 survey depicts a public that overwhelmingly regards the status quo as unfair, remains open to more than one constitutional model, strongly favours an active negotiation route, and has limited everyday contact with Turkish Cypriots despite a moderately positive affective climate.

92.4%

judge the status quo unfair.

75.3%

accept or tolerate BBF.

82.8%

prefer an active negotiation route.

51.7%

report no contact with Turkish Cypriots.

Publication boundary. Hopes and concerns are presented only at baseline, before exposure to pro/anti-BBF arguments. The public platform does not publish post-communication shifts, message testing, psychological targeting profiles, persuasion-oriented predictors, political-leadership/gender modules or presidential conjoint analysis. Article 4 remains a stand-alone newspaper article and has no detailed public web module.

Public evidence 03

Process

More than eight in ten respondents choose an active route rather than non-resumption. The largest single group favours restarting from Crans-Montana, while substantial minorities favour citizens' assemblies or staged implementation.

Preferred route for the peace process

Restart from Crans-Montana38.7%
Citizens' assemblies22.2%
Staged / interim implementation21.9%
Do not restart17.2%

82.8% in total prefer one of the active negotiation routes.
